## Purpose

Workers' comp is a **separate, state-administered system** with its own agency, deadlines, and benefit types — very different from third-party PI. Many firms either handle comp directly or, more often, spot the **comp + third-party** overlap and coordinate. This article frames the department and the coordination; comp practice is **highly state-specific** — verify your state's act and deadlines.

## Comp basics (verify by state)

- Comp is generally **no-fault** for work-related injuries: it pays medical and a portion of lost wages (indemnity), regardless of employer fault, in exchange for limits on suing the employer.
- Strict **reporting and claim deadlines** apply (notice to employer, claim filing) — calendar them immediately; they differ from PI statutes of limitations.
- Benefit types typically include medical, temporary disability, permanent disability/impairment rating, and vocational elements — names and formulas vary by state.

## The third-party overlap (where PI firms add value)

- If someone **other than the employer** caused the on-the-job injury (e.g., a negligent driver during a work errand, a defective product, a subcontractor), the worker may have **both** a comp claim and a **third-party PI claim**.
- The comp carrier usually has a **lien / right of reimbursement** against the third-party recovery for what it paid. Resolving that lien (and any credit/offset against future comp) is central to netting the client a fair recovery — treat it like the [subrogation](29-health-insurance-subrogation.md) workflow, but under your state's comp statute.
- Coordinate timing: the third-party case runs on the PI [lifecycle](00-case-lifecycle-and-workflow-map.md); the comp claim runs on its own track.

## Intake flags

At [intake](35-lead-intake-and-signup-operations.md), always ask: *were you working when this happened?* If yes, flag for comp analysis, capture employer/carrier info and the claim number, and confirm reporting deadlines are met.

## Attorney gate

Whether to take a comp matter, how to structure a comp + third-party case, and comp-lien resolution/credit strategy are **attorney decisions** and often require comp-specialist counsel. Do not advise on comp benefits without qualified review.
