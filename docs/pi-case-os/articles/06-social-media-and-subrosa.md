## What clients sign

Intake includes an **internet surveillance** acknowledgment. Case managers must explain it in plain language, not bury it in a stack of signatures.

## Subrosa

On larger cases, carriers may hire investigators for **subrosa** video — surveillance of the client’s daily activities. Training associates this risk with higher-exposure matters (example threshold discussed: cases over about **$100,000**).

Investigators look for activity inconsistent with claimed injuries: heavy lifting, sports, dancing, physically demanding work without the brace the client wears to appointments.

## Social media rules from training

- Do **not** post photos or videos of activities the client claims they can no longer do
- Ordinary posts about daily life are less risky than athletic or physically impressive content
- Do **not** delete posts to hide them — training calls deletion **spoliation of evidence** and treats it as worse than the original post if discovered
- Assume passwords and accounts may be requested in litigation

## How to say it

Keep the tone practical: “If this becomes a large case, they may follow you for a week. Don’t hand them a highlight reel that contradicts what you tell the doctors.”
