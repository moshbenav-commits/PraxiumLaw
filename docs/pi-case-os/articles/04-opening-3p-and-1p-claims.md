# Opening Third-Party and First-Party Claims

**Category:** Insurance Communication Rules  
**Source:** Personal Injury Workflow; Case Manage / Cleaner Fish; claims training modules  
**Audience:** Case managers

---

## Day-one third-party (3P) claim

Training requires opening the **3P** claim (or identifying the existing claim number) on **day one**, then sending a **Letter of Representation (LOR)** with the signed general release page.

### Information to capture

- Adjuster name, phone, fax, email, mailing address
- Claim number and policy number
- Personal vs commercial policy
- Whether BI and property damage are the **same** adjuster or split
- Whether **driver and owner** are the same person (if not, there may be additional coverage)

### Do not provide on the open call

Do not give the carrier the client’s SSN, date of birth, home address, or phone number when opening the claim.

### After the LOR

- Save the sent LOR in the file
- If no acknowledgment in about **five days**, call
- Push for **liability acceptance within about 30 days** — doctors will eventually ask for approval on expensive care (for example injections), and training does not want that spend without liability posture

## First-party (1P) is last resort

Open 1P (collision, rental, MedPay/PIP, UM/UIM as applicable) when the client cannot wait on 3P — for example no car to get to work and no alternate vehicle.

### Why training avoids early 1P

Carriers talk. If 1P and 3P share history, prior accidents and pre-existing conditions can be used to reduce the injury claim. Training compares it to gossip between friends: information given to one side reaches the other.

### MedPay timing

- Submit medical bills up to MedPay limits when appropriate
- Send notice so providers do not take MedPay directly in ways that hurt later negotiation
- Open/send MedPay earlier if a hospital is likely to bill MedPay and get paid in full before the firm controls it
- Otherwise, MedPay often waits until 1P is intentionally opened

## Subject lines matter

Faxes and emails should include the **client name** and document type (for example “Third Party LOR”) so staff can search later without opening twenty attachments.
