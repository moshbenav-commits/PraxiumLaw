# Property Damage Timing and Liability Acceptance

**Category:** Insurance Communication · Client Coaching  
**Recommended procedure** — cross-reference against your firm's policy and jurisdiction before use.  
**White-label:** Any firm. See [`../DISCLOSURE.md`](../DISCLOSURE.md).  
**References:** [`../REFERENCES.md`](../REFERENCES.md)  
**Audience:** Case managers

---

Clients often care about the car before they care about the MRI. Training treats property damage (PD) as both a **client-retention** issue and a **liability** issue.

## The core message

The at-fault carrier rarely accepts liability on day one. They often wait for:

- Their insured’s statement
- The police report (commonly **two to three weeks** to become available)

Until liability is accepted, the 3P carrier is unlikely to authorize repairs, total-loss payment, or rental on favorable terms.

## What to tell the client

Explain that the car will not be fixed “right away” through the other driver’s insurance. If they need a vehicle immediately for work, discuss opening a **first-party (1P)** claim under their own collision/rental coverage — and explain that coverage only exists if they paid for it (“you get what you pay for”).

Training cautions staff not to volunteer that liability fights can last **months** in a way that panics the client, while still being honest that acceptance is not instant.

## Total loss rule of thumb

Med training notes: if repairs cost more than about **60%** of the vehicle’s current market value, expect a total-loss analysis.

## Estimates

Prefer a repair estimate from a **referral body shop**, not only the insurer’s appraiser. Training states insurer estimates are often low, and low PD numbers are later used to argue “minor impact” against the injury claim. Example cited: **$500** insurer figure versus **$5,000** at an independent shop.

## Loss of use

If the client is without a vehicle and without a rental, pursue **loss of use**: days without the car × daily rental cost of a **similar** vehicle (not a cheaper class).

## Why PD is a firing risk

Training is blunt: clients fire firms when rental and repairs are ignored. When PD is active, follow up **daily** until rental or repairs move — not on the same leisurely cadence as a routine treatment check-in.
