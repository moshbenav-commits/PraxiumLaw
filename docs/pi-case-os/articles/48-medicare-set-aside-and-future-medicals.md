## Purpose

Past medical liens are only half the picture ([subrogation, art. 29](29-health-insurance-subrogation.md)). When a client will need **future** injury-related care — or receives **means-tested benefits** — the settlement must be structured to protect Medicare's and Medicaid's interests and the client's eligibility. These are specialist areas; **use an MSA/settlement-planning vendor and qualified counsel.** Screen every case; act on the ones that trigger.

## Screen every case for three risks

1. **Medicare interests (future care):** Is the client a **Medicare beneficiary** or likely to enroll soon (e.g., 62+, on/applying for SSDI, ESRD)? If future injury-related care is expected, **Medicare's interest in future medicals must be considered** under the Medicare Secondary Payer Act.
2. **Means-tested benefits:** Does the client receive **Medicaid or SSI**? A lump sum can **disqualify** them unless protected.
3. **Vulnerable/large recoveries:** minors, incapacity, or large sums that shouldn't be handed over as a lump.

## Medicare Set-Aside (MSA)

- An **MSA** carves out settlement funds to pay future injury-related care Medicare would otherwise cover, so Medicare stays secondary.
- Well-established in **workers' comp (WCMSA)**, with CMS review thresholds; **liability MSAs (LMSA)** are less formalized — CMS has no finalized LMSA review process, but the underlying MSP obligation to protect Medicare still exists. **Confirm current CMS guidance** ([CMS](https://www.cms.gov/medicare/coordination-benefits-recovery/attorney-services)).
- Use an **MSA vendor** to evaluate/allocate; document the analysis even when no set-aside is ultimately required.

## Structured settlements

- Instead of a lump sum, an **annuity** pays out over time — useful for minors, large cases, and future-care needs; can offer tax advantages and prevent dissipation.
- Common with [minor's compromise](30-minors-compromise-and-court-approval.md) (paired with blocked accounts) and MSAs (a structured MSA funds future care over life expectancy).

## Special Needs Trust (SNT)

- For clients on **SSI/Medicaid**, a properly drafted **(d)(4)(A) special-needs trust** can hold the recovery **without** losing benefits.
- Must be established and administered correctly — **drafting is a job for qualified counsel**, not staff.

## Workflow

1. **Screen** at intake and again before settlement (benefits status, future-care outlook).
2. **Flag** any trigger to the attorney; engage MSA/structured-settlement/SNT specialists early — they need lead time.
3. **Coordinate** the past-lien resolution ([29](29-health-insurance-subrogation.md)) with the future-care plan.
4. **Document** Medicare-interest consideration in the file regardless of outcome.
5. **Reflect** any set-aside/structure/trust in the [disbursement](25-disbursement-sheet-preparation.md) and closing.

## Attorney gate

MSA necessity, structured-settlement design, and SNT drafting are **attorney/specialist decisions**. Do not disburse to a Medicaid/SSI recipient or a Medicare beneficiary with future care without this analysis. Not legal or benefits advice — confirm current CMS rules and your state's Medicaid rules with qualified counsel.
