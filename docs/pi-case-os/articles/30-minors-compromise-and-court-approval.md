# Minor's Compromise & Court Approval

## Purpose

A settlement of a minor's claim generally is **not binding until a court approves it**. This procedure covers the petition, approval, and safeguarding of the funds. Thresholds, forms, guardian-ad-litem rules, and account rules are **state- and often county-specific** — confirm your local rule (see references).

## Who and when

- A minor cannot contract or settle their own claim. An eligible adult (typically a parent with custody, or an appointed guardian of the estate) petitions **on behalf of** the minor.
- Court approval is commonly required when a minor's net recovery exceeds a **state-set dollar threshold** or when suit was filed; some jurisdictions appoint a **guardian ad litem** to review the settlement's adequacy and report to the court.
- Watch the **statute of limitations** — many states toll it for minors, but do not assume; calendar it.

## Documents to gather (redact minor PII)

- Statement of facts and date of loss.
- Certified birth certificate (confirm the minor is under the age of majority **when the petition is filed**).
- The minor's Social Security number (needed to open a blocked account).
- Medical bills and records, and **approved reductions**, attached as exhibits — **redacted**.

## The petition (typical required contents)

Draft the petition to set out: the minor's name, age, and residence; the circumstances making it a disputed claim; the third party; date/place/facts of the accident; parents/guardian and who has custody; the petitioner and their relationship to the minor; the total proceeds and their apportionment (attorney fees, medical, other costs) and whether fees/costs come before or after any contingency calculation; the petitioner's best-interest statement; and an acknowledgment that acceptance bars further claims against the settling party.

## Filing & approval flow (adapt to local practice)

1. Generate the petition + civil cover sheet with **redacted** exhibits; **attorney reviews**.
2. E-file per the court's system; provide any courtesy copy the court prefers.
3. On approval, prepare the order; obtain the judge's signature; e-file the signed order.
4. Obtain a **certified copy** of the order.

## Safeguarding the funds (blocked account)

- Funds are typically placed in a **blocked/court-restricted account** at an FDIC-insured institution, inaccessible until the minor reaches majority (or by further court order for the minor's benefit).
- The guardian usually must open the account and **file proof** (receipt + confirmation) within the court's deadline (e.g., 60 days). Missing it can carry serious consequences for the guardian.
- File the proof, then take any approval hearing off calendar per local procedure.

## Attorney gate

Fee/cost apportionment, the petition, and the order are **attorney-approved**. Do not disburse a minor's funds outside the court-approved structure.
