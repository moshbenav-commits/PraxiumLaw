# Why Clients Should Retrieve Their Own Hospital Bills

**Category:** Medical Record Tracking · Client Coaching  
**Recommended procedure** — cross-reference against your firm's policy and jurisdiction before use.  
**White-label:** Any firm. See [`../DISCLOSURE.md`](../DISCLOSURE.md).  
**References:** [`../REFERENCES.md`](../REFERENCES.md)  
**Audience:** Case managers  
**Sensitivity:** Strategy coaching — attorney review before external publish

---

One of the most counter-intuitive intake rules in the training: **do not rush to order hospital bills and records under the firm’s letterhead** if the client can get them.

## The problem

Hospitals and contracted billing groups often treat attorney-involved auto cases differently from self-pay or insured patients. Training explains that facilities may expect full billed charges when they see a law firm on the request, and may be more willing to discuss reductions when they believe the patient is collecting records personally.

## What one ER visit actually produces

Clients are often confused by multiple bills from one visit. Training breaks it into four streams:

1. **Hospital facility** bill (space, nursing, facility fees)
2. **ER physician** group (contracted doctors — different company name on the envelope)
3. **Radiology** group (another contracted entity)
4. **Ambulance** (often arrives by mail later)

Tell the client to save **everything** that arrives at home and bring copies in.

## The coaching script (paraphrased from training)

Explain that you are not ignoring their bills. You want them to obtain facility records so that, at the end of the case, they may negotiate a better balance than if every provider already coded the file as attorney-driven. Give them about **a month** of reminders.

## When the firm takes over

If the client will not retrieve records, the firm eventually requests them anyway — training is explicit that the case cannot wait forever for demand. The tradeoff: reduction leverage may be worse. After roughly a month of non-compliance, staff order records and document that the client was given the chance to self-retrieve.

## Health insurance at the hospital

When the client has health insurance, training directs staff to tell the client to **present the card** at the hospital and ambulance so benefits are billed appropriately. That interacts with later subrogation workflow (open subro only when benefits were actually used, with Medicare as a special case).
