# Client Termination & Attorney Liens

**White-label:** Any firm. See [`../DISCLOSURE.md`](../DISCLOSURE.md).
**References:** [`../REFERENCES.md`](../REFERENCES.md) — Model Rule 1.16; attorney charging liens & quantum meruit

## Purpose

How to withdraw from (or "drop") a matter properly, and — where permitted — preserve the firm's right to be paid for work already done. Withdrawal implicates ethics rules; treat this as **attorney-directed** work.

## Before deciding to drop

Confirm there is truly nothing to work with, because withdrawing has costs and duties:

- **Coverage check:** Does the client have UM/UIM? Anyone in their household? Anyone whose mail they share? Has the defendant been put on notice to disclose coverage?
- **Contact attempts:** Try the client and the emergency contact; send a "contact the office" letter before concluding the client is unreachable.
- Escalate to the supervising attorney **early** — the decision to withdraw is theirs.

## Withdrawing the right way (Model Rule 1.16)

Upon terminating, take reasonable steps to protect the client's interests: give reasonable notice, allow time to find new counsel, surrender the file/property the client is entitled to, and refund any unearned advance. Courts and bar rules limit a firm's ability to withdraw and still get paid without a legitimate reason — document it.

- **If the client fired the firm:** no drop letter needed from you; they sent one.
- **If the firm withdraws:** send the appropriate disengagement letter — not asserting a lien, asserting a percentage lien, or asserting a fixed-dollar lien.

## Attorney charging lien / quantum meruit (where allowed)

Whether a firm can assert a lien — and whether a *withdrawing* (vs. *discharged*) attorney can — **varies by state**. Verify your jurisdiction. General framework:

- A discharged contingency-fee attorney is usually limited to **quantum meruit** — the reasonable value of services rendered to the date of discharge — even though the fee was contingent.
- **Perfect the lien** as your state requires — commonly written notice, served in person or by certified mail / return receipt, on the client and the party against whom the client has a claim. Many firms send an early lien letter at intake so the defendant's address is still good.

### Building the lien amount (quantum meruit worksheet)

- Reconstruct every time entry from the activity log, document store, and communications — chronological, past tense, third person.
- Apply your firm's role rates (e.g., attorney vs. staff) and a minimum increment (e.g., 0.2 hr). Bill real work (opening claims, drafting letters, requesting reports) — none of it happened "magically."
- Split time common to multiple clients in one vehicle across those clients.
- Redact anything that could harm the client (e.g., missed appointments) — the lien may be seen by others.
- **Attorney reviews and approves** the itemized lien before it is generated or served.

## Serve the drop/lien letter

Send certified, return receipt requested, to the parties your state requires — typically the client (unless they signed the lien in person), the client's carrier, the defendant (skip-trace if needed), the defendant's carrier, and any new counsel. Save proof of service to the file.

## Attorney gate

Withdrawal, lien assertion, and lien amount are **attorney decisions**. Confirm state ethics rules and lien statutes before acting.
