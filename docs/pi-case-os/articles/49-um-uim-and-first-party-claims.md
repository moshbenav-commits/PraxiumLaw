## Purpose

When the at-fault driver has **no** insurance or **not enough**, the client's own **uninsured/underinsured motorist (UM/UIM)** coverage is often the real source of recovery. First-party claims run differently from third-party claims — the client's own carrier becomes the payer (and can become adverse). **UM/UIM rules are highly state-specific** ([coverage reference](33-auto-insurance-coverage-reference.md)); confirm yours.

## UM vs. UIM

- **UM** — the at-fault driver has no liability coverage (or is a hit-and-run/phantom vehicle, where allowed).
- **UIM** — the at-fault driver has coverage, but **less than the client's damages**. UIM fills the gap above the 3P limits.

## Two things that will burn you if missed

1. **Setoff / offset vs. excess (state-specific):** some states reduce UIM by the 3P payment ("setoff/gap" states); others let UIM stack **on top** ("excess/add-on" states). This changes whether a UIM claim is even worth pursuing — analyze it early.
2. **Consent to settle with the 3P:** most UIM policies require the client to **get the UIM carrier's written consent before settling** (and releasing) the at-fault driver — settling without it can **destroy the UIM claim** and the carrier's subrogation against the tortfeasor. Always request consent (and give the carrier its short window to advance/waive) **before** signing the 3P release.

## Stacking

Where allowed, multiple policies or vehicles may **stack** to increase available UM/UIM limits. Check every household policy and vehicle ([coverage reference](33-auto-insurance-coverage-reference.md)).

## Working a first-party claim

- Open the UM/UIM claim with the client's carrier; provide notice per the policy.
- Treat the client's own carrier professionally but remember it may become **adverse** — document everything ([correspondence](34-correspondence-and-communications.md)).
- Build and present the claim like a [demand](39-demand-letter-drafting.md); UM/UIM disputes often go to **arbitration** per the policy rather than court.

## Bad-faith / policy-limits time-limit demands

- A **policy-limits demand with a reasonable deadline** (well-documented, with the proof the carrier needs to evaluate) can set up **bad-faith exposure** if a carrier unreasonably fails to settle within limits — potentially opening recovery **above** the policy limit.
- Requirements and effect are **state-specific and attorney-driven**; document the demand, the proof provided, and the carrier's response meticulously.

## Attorney gate

Setoff analysis, consent-to-settle strategy, policy-limits/bad-faith demands, and arbitration are **attorney decisions**. Never release the 3P without confirming the UIM consequences.
