## Purpose

A plain-language reference to the coverages that fund an auto personal-injury recovery, so intake and case staff ask the right questions and don't miss a source of payment. **Coverage names, requirements, and rules vary by state** (tort vs. no-fault, stacking, minimum limits) — confirm your state.

## The coverages that matter

| Coverage | What it does |
|----------|--------------|
| **Bodily Injury Liability (3P)** | The at-fault driver's coverage that pays the injured claimant; the primary recovery source. |
| **UM / UIM (1P)** | The client's own uninsured/underinsured motorist coverage — pays when the at-fault driver has no or insufficient coverage. Often the difference-maker; always check for it. |
| **MedPay** | First-party coverage for medical bills regardless of fault (common in tort states). |
| **PIP (No-Fault)** | First-party medical/wage coverage in no-fault states, with state-specific thresholds to step outside no-fault. |
| **Property Damage Liability** | Pays for vehicle damage — handle before signing bodily-injury releases (see [`03-property-damage-liability-timing.md`](03-property-damage-liability-timing.md)). |

## Questions to resolve on every auto case

- Does the client have **UM/UIM**? Does anyone in their household, or anyone whose mail they share? Was UM/UIM validly **waived** (an invalid waiver may restore coverage in some states)?
- Is there **MedPay/PIP**, and what are the limits?
- What are the **liability limits** on the at-fault policy, and are there multiple/umbrella policies or multiple at-fault parties?
- Does your state allow **stacking** of multiple policies or vehicles?

## Fault, briefly

Liability turns on negligence and each state's comparative/contributory-fault rule, which can reduce or bar recovery. Rear-end and left-turn-across-traffic collisions commonly (not always) point to the following/turning driver; a police report and physical evidence still control. Fault determinations are **attorney/adjuster** analyses, not staff conclusions.

## Attorney gate

Coverage analysis and any advice about a client's own policy are **attorney functions**. Use this reference to gather facts, not to advise clients.
