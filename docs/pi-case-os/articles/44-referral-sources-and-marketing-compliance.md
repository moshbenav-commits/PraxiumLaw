# Referral Sources & Marketing Compliance

**Category:** Compliance / Ethics
**Recommended procedure** — cross-reference against your firm's policy and jurisdiction before use.
**White-label:** Any firm. See [`../DISCLOSURE.md`](../DISCLOSURE.md).
**References:** [`../REFERENCES.md`](../REFERENCES.md) — solicitation, fee-splitting, kickbacks

## Purpose

How the firm may — and may not — acquire clients and interact with medical providers. These are **criminal-exposure** areas in many states, including where a technology platform (e.g., a coordination app) is involved. When in doubt, **don't pay for the introduction** and route it to a supervising attorney.

## Bright-line prohibitions (verify your state; several carry felony/serious penalties)

- **No paying for client referrals ("capping"/"runners").** Paying a non-lawyer to steer accident victims to the firm is illegal in many states; a client solicited this way may be able to **void the fee agreement** for a period of years. (Nevada example: NRS 7.045.)
- **No fee-splitting with non-lawyers.** The firm cannot share legal fees with a non-lawyer person or company (ABA Model Rule 5.4), except in the few states that license alternative business structures.
- **No pay-for-patient / pay-for-referral with medical providers.** Paying or receiving anything of value for patient referrals between the firm and providers is prohibited (state fee-splitting/anti-kickback/patient-brokering laws; federal AKS/Stark where federal healthcare dollars are involved).
- **No improper solicitation.** Direct in-person/live solicitation of accident victims is restricted; follow your state's advertising and solicitation rules.

## What is allowed (general framework — confirm locally)

- **Advertising** that complies with your state's rules (truthful, non-misleading, with required disclaimers).
- **Client-initiated** contact and genuine word-of-mouth referrals from past clients.
- **Reciprocal lawyer referrals** only as your ethics rules permit (often with client disclosure and consent; division of fees typically must be proportional or by written client-consented agreement — Model Rule 1.5(e)).
- **Neutral technology tools** that a client already chose to use, charged at **flat, fair-market fees** for real services — not per-client or per-referral compensation.

## Working with medical providers

- Coordinate care and records **without** exchanging value for referrals.
- Letters of protection and lien arrangements are about **payment for services rendered**, not referral fees — keep them clean and documented.
- Do not condition provider relationships on volume of referred patients.

## Technology / platform intake (e.g., PraxHQ)

If the firm uses a coordination platform, it must operate as a **neutral tool**, not a paid feeder:

- Clients **self-select**; providers are presented neutrally.
- Platform fees are **flat/fair-market SaaS or administrative**, never a share of recovery or a per-referral payment.
- The platform gives **no legal advice** and does not negotiate — attorney gates apply.
- See [`../../praxhq/LEGAL_REGULATORY_RESEARCH.md`](../../praxhq/LEGAL_REGULATORY_RESEARCH.md) and [`38-firm-operations-and-office-policies.md`](38-firm-operations-and-office-policies.md).

## Attorney gate

Intake-source arrangements, referral relationships, marketing copy, and any provider or platform agreement must be **reviewed and approved by a licensed attorney** for your jurisdiction before use. This article is compliance education, not legal advice.
