# Policy Limits, Claimants, and Driver vs Owner

**Category:** Insurance Communication · Settlement Prep  
**Source:** Personal Injury Workflow; Case Manage / Cleaner Fish; med training notes  
**Audience:** Case managers

---

## Get limits early — but cheaply when possible

Training prefers limits disclosed on the carrier’s acknowledgment or LOR response **before** paying for a formal limits pull (costs cited in the **$80–$250+** range).

Pull sooner when:

- There is **no police report**
- The acknowledgment does not disclose limits
- Liability posture requires knowing exposure before approving expensive care

## Record limits on every related file

When multiple claimants share a loss, enter the same limit stack on **each** related case. Include:

- 3P bodily injury limits (for example 25/50, 100/300)
- UM / UIM
- MedPay / PIP
- Number of **claimants** splitting the limits (for pro rata awareness)

## Driver vs owner

If the adverse **driver** is not the **owner**, there may be more than one policy. Insurance may respond first on the owner’s vehicle policy, then on the driver’s. Always ask whether driver and owner match.

## Coverage vocabulary from med training notes

- Nevada minimum example discussed: **25/50**
- Additional common stacks: 50/100, 100/300, 250/500, 1M
- UIM (uninsured / underinsured motorist)
- Collision
- MedPay / PIP (PIP noted as WA-required in notes)

## How limits change the client conversation

When clients compare their timeline to a coworker’s settlement, training directs staff to reframe: different limits, different injuries, different body types, different treatment — not a single “normal” duration or dollar amount.
