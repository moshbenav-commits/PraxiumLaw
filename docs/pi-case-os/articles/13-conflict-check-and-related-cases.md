## Conflict check

Before the firm is locked into a case, run a **conflict check** on the defendant. If the driver and the owner of the adverse vehicle are different people, also conflict-check the **owner** — training notes that insurance may follow the vehicle/owner policy first and the driver’s policy second, and that prior representation of either party creates a conflict.

Training allows delaying the owner conflict until the police report confirms identities (about **15 days**), but the defendant check is day-one work.

## Related cases for vehicle occupants

When multiple people in the same vehicle hire the firm:

1. Create a **primary** project (usually the driver; sometimes a passenger if the driver is at fault or represented elsewhere)
2. Create **secondary** projects for other clients
3. **Link** related cases
4. Obtain a **conflict of interest waiver** signed by each client

The waiver addresses the risk that co-clients later blame each other. Training references ethical limits on representing conflicting parties without written waiver.

## Why defendant data quality matters

Enter adverse parties carefully. If the firm later represents someone who was the defendant in a prior matter, the system must surface that conflict. Incomplete defendant records create silent conflicts.
