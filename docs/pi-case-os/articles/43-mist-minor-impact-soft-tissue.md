## Purpose

"MIST" (Minor Impact Soft Tissue) is the insurance-industry label for low-property-damage collisions with soft-tissue injuries. Carriers defend these aggressively with a **"low impact = low injury"** argument. This article is the handling strategy; the case still runs the standard [lifecycle](00-case-lifecycle-and-workflow-map.md).

## Why carriers fight MIST cases

- Low visible vehicle damage is used to argue the forces were too small to injure.
- Soft-tissue injuries (strains/sprains) often don't show on imaging, so documentation quality carries the case.
- Some carriers route these to special low-value or litigation units.

## Handling strategy (documentation wins)

- **Photograph both vehicles** and preserve repair estimates — low PD is not the same as no PD; capture what damage exists.
- **Consistent, contemporaneous treatment** matters most: early evaluation, no unexplained gaps, consistent symptom reporting ([treatment compliance](16-treatment-compliance-coaching.md), [gaps & MRI timing](05-treatment-gaps-and-mri-timing.md)).
- Ensure the medical records clearly connect mechanism of injury to complaints; a clean [records file](08-meds-tab-and-record-hygiene.md) is the demand's backbone.
- Be realistic about value and set **client expectations early** — these resolve for less and can require suit to move.
- Keep spend proportionate to available [coverage/limits](15-policy-limits-and-claimants.md); over-treatment relative to a small policy hurts the client's net.

## When to escalate

If the carrier will not move despite solid documentation, evaluate [litigation](31-litigation-handoff-and-management.md) — sometimes filing is the only way to reach fair value. This is an **attorney call**.

## Attorney gate

Case valuation, treatment-spend guidance relative to liability posture, and the decision to litigate a MIST case are **attorney decisions**. Staff document thoroughly and flag; they do not set value.
