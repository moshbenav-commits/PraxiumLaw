# PraxiumLaw PI Case OS — Public References

Public, authoritative sources that inform the recommended procedures in [`articles/`](./articles/). These are provided **for orientation only**. They are general and often federal or model-rule level; **state law and your jurisdiction's ethics rules control**, and rules change. Confirm current authority before relying on any procedure. See [`DISCLOSURE.md`](./DISCLOSURE.md).

> Procedures in this knowledge base are original, firm-neutral write-ups of standard industry practice. No third-party firm's proprietary materials are reproduced. The sources below are cited because they document the underlying public law and widely published practice.

## Professional responsibility & ethics (model rules)

- ABA Model Rule 1.5 — Fees (contingent-fee agreements must be in writing) — https://www.americanbar.org/groups/professional_responsibility/publications/model_rules_of_professional_conduct/
- ABA Model Rule 1.15 — Safekeeping Property (client trust accounts; keep client funds separate; records retained, commonly 5 years) — https://www.americanbar.org/groups/professional_responsibility/publications/model_rules_of_professional_conduct/rule_1_15_safekeeping_property/
- ABA Model Rule 1.16 — Declining or Terminating Representation (notice, protect client interests, return file/unearned fees) — https://www.americanbar.org/groups/professional_responsibility/publications/model_rules_of_professional_conduct/rule_1_16_declining_or_terminating_representation/

*Every state adopts its own version of these rules. Use your state bar's rules of professional conduct, not the model text.*

## Attorney liens & quantum meruit

- Attorney Charging Liens: A Primer (J. Martin) — https://www.sfgh.com/siteFiles/News/Attorney%20Charging%20Liens.pdf
- North Carolina State Bar — The Problem with Attorney Charging Liens — https://www.ncbar.gov/for-lawyers/ethics/ethics-articles/the-problem-with-attorney-charging-liens/
- Lien guide for the trial attorney (Advocate Magazine, Parts 1–2) — https://www.advocatemagazine.com/article/2018-october/lien-guide-for-the-trial-attorney

*General principle: a discharged conting-fee attorney is typically limited to quantum meruit (reasonable value of services to date). Whether a withdrawing attorney may assert a charging lien varies by state and by cause for withdrawal.*

## Medical liens & subrogation

- CMS — Medicare's Recovery Process (conditional payments / MSP) — https://www.cms.gov/medicare/coordination-benefits-recovery/beneficiary-services/recovery-process
- CMS — Attorney Services / Conditional Payment Information (MSPRP portal) — https://www.cms.gov/medicare/coordination-benefits-recovery/attorney-services
- ERISA subrogation & the made-whole doctrine; *US Airways v. McCutchen*, 569 U.S. 88 (2013) — https://www.buchanandisability.com/erisa-health-care-subrogation-reimbursement-and-overpayments/

*Notes: Medicare's low-dollar liability threshold (physical-trauma settlements of $750 or less) and reporting timelines change periodically — verify on cms.gov. ERISA "made-whole" is a default that clear plan language can override; check the plan document and controlling circuit.*

## Minor's settlements (court approval)

- Washington SPR 98.16W — Settlement of Claims of Minors (example court rule) — https://www.courts.wa.gov/court_rules/pdf/SPR/SUP_SPR_98_16W_00.pdf
- Illinois 16th Judicial Circuit — Article 10: Settlements Involving Minors' Proceeds — https://www.illinois16thjudicialcircuit.org/Documents/localCourtRules/article10.pdf
- Overview — how minor personal-injury settlements are handled (guardian ad litem, blocked account) — https://www.parkchenaur.com/resources/what-to-expect/litigation-overview/minor-settlements/

*Court-approval dollar thresholds, guardian-ad-litem requirements, and blocked-account rules are set by each state and often each county. Confirm the local rule.*

## Statutes of limitations

- Personal Injury Statute of Limitations by State — https://www.martinianlaw.com/personal-injury-statute-of-limitations-by-state/
- Statute of Limitations in All 50 States (MWL Law) — https://www.mwl-law.com/resources/statute-limitations-50-states/

*Most states use a 2- or 3-year window for personal injury; a few range from 1 to 6 years, with tolling for minors and discovery-rule exceptions. Always calendar to the specific state and claim type.*
