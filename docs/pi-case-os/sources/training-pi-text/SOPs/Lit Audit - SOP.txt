Other Important Notes:
COR
	•	If a case is transferring to lit, CMs should start requesting bills, records, and COR. It certifies the complete and accurate representation of the documents within its control.
 EAC/ECC Docs
	•	also called NRCP 16.1 Docs is a list of witnesses and documents that we are going to introduce at trial or arbitration.
	•	Once the ECC is held, we have to file and serve JCCR.
	•	We have to make sure we have the CORs and if we have time, start requesting so that when we file ECC we have these CORs.
	•	If the VAs have extra time then do the EAC docs and the Discovery.  If they are busy it can wait.
Discovery
	•	You want to scare the defendant.  Perception is everything.  There is a lot to be said when your work product (letters and pleadings look professional and do not have typos and grammatical issues) is flawless and timely.  That shows that you are a formidable attorney and someone that they should be scared of as you are on top of things.  Day one and you are serving discovery.  As a new attorney, my boss said she spent extra few minutes remembering things.  These extra few minutes was worth it because at court hearings she would say, "No no no your Honor, flip to page 3 line 52 of their motion, they stated blah blah blah."  Because she had a memory of a steel trap, people were afraid to litigate against her because they were afraid of losing.  You serving your discovery early and with little to no typos and grammatical errors shows that you mean business.
	•	Why you want to serve discovery early.  The other reason is that you want to see the Defense's flaws early on.  Perhaps they can no longer find their defendant.  Perhaps in answering the questions they realize that their client was hopped up on drugs or alcohol the day of the crash.  These are flaws that they do not want to admit, and perhaps reasons for them to settle with us.
	•	In litigation, Plaintiff wants to hurry and get paid, Defendant wants to drag things along and delay and defend.  Time value of money, we want to finish our cases sooner rather than later, get the money and move on to our next case.
	•	If we got served with discovery, we need to see if the questions really differ from the questions that we have in the lit letter.
