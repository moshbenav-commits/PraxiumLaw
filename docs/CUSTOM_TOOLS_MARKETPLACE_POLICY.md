# Praxium Law — Custom tools & marketplace policy (quick note)

**Status:** Product + legal direction — **draft for attorney review before Terms of Service / MSA ship.**  
**Last updated:** 2026-06-24

---

## Summary

When a firm pays Praxium to build a **custom tool**, we still **add that tool to the marketplace** for other agencies. The paying firm keeps early access and direct benefit; Praxium retains platform ownership. As the tool creates value across the network, we **share upside** with the originating firm and **reduce their monthly spend** — a thank-you for making the product better.

---

## Business rules

### 1. Paid custom development → still marketplace

- Customer pays for scoping, design, and build of a firm-specific workflow or integration.
- On delivery, the tool is **enabled for that firm** and **listed in the Praxium marketplace** (other firms may adopt it subject to pricing/access rules).
- Paying for development is **not** a buyout of the tool or underlying IP.

### 2. Profit sharing (when traction proves network value)

Trigger when the tool meets agreed adoption/usage thresholds (TBD — e.g. N active agencies, M monthly runs, or $X marketplace revenue).

| Phase | Originating firm | Praxium |
|-------|------------------|---------|
| Build | Pays dev fee; gets tool first | Builds, hosts, owns IP |
| Early use | Full access; standard subscription | Marketplace listing live |
| Network value | **Profit share** on third-party usage/revenue | Platform + ops margin |
| Maturity | **Lower monthly subscription** on a defined schedule | Scale via marketplace |

**Intent:** Firms with the ideas help the whole ecosystem; we reward them when their contribution scales beyond their own shop.

### 3. Subscription relief (“thank you” tier)

- As third-party adoption grows, create a **written reduction plan** for the originating firm’s monthly spend (credits, tier downgrade, or revenue offset — TBD in contract).
- Frame explicitly: *you helped make Praxium better for everyone* — not a refund of the original dev fee unless separately negotiated.

## IP & ownership — legal direction (Apple-style)

**Principle:** Use of the Praxium platform, workflows, and product surface creates **platform-derived work**. Even when a customer **conceives** a feature or workflow, **Praxium builds, integrates, and owns** the resulting tool, code, models, templates, and marketplace listing — similar in spirit to how employers/platform operators treat inventions developed in the course of using company systems (cf. Apple / standard **IP assignment** and **work made for hire** clauses).

### Draft disclaimer language (not final — counsel must edit)

> **Custom tools & marketplace.** If you request custom development on Praxium Law (“Custom Tool”), you acknowledge that (a) the idea, request, or business requirement may originate with you or your firm, but (b) all implementation — including software, configurations, prompts, automations, UI, documentation, and derivatives — is developed by Praxium and remains **Praxium’s exclusive property**. Payment for Custom Tool development is a **development and priority-access fee**, not a license buyout or assignment to you. Praxium may add Custom Tools to the marketplace for other subscribers. When a Custom Tool generates measurable value for other firms, Praxium may offer **revenue sharing** and **subscription adjustments** to the originating firm under a separate written program; such benefits are discretionary until defined in an addendum or marketplace policy. By using the platform or submitting a custom tool request, you assign to Praxium all right, title, and interest in platform-integrated deliverables to the maximum extent permitted by law, and you waive any claim that conception alone entitles you to ownership of the built product.

> **Platform environment.** You understand that working inside Praxium — including exposure to existing tools, templates, and workflows — may inform new feature ideas. Any tool built on or integrated with Praxium infrastructure is treated as **work product of the platform relationship**, not standalone IP retained by the requesting firm, except where Praxium explicitly agrees in a signed **IP carve-out addendum** (rare; enterprise only).
